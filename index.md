---
title: Sanctioned Iranian tankers vanish from Chinese crude terminals, but gas tankers still dock at Chaozhou
---

157 different US-sanctioned tankers called at Qingdao before they were sanctioned. In the last 12 months, after their sanctions, none did. The one place in China where sanctioned ships still tied up at a pier with their location signal on was Chaozhou, in Guangdong, and most of those were gas carriers.

## Executive summary

- **Terminals:** Sinopec Kantons (HKEX 00934) is the Hong Kong-listed company most exposed to US sanctions on Chinese oil terminals. The sanctioned Rizhao terminal it half-owns made about 12% of its 2024 pre-tax profit and is now being liquidated.
- **Tracking:** In 220,000 dated port visits by 669 sanctioned ships, none tied up at a Qingdao, Rizhao, Ningbo, Tianjin or Huizhou pier in the last 12 months. 7 tied up at Chaozhou's Jinshiwan port area between October 2025 and July 2026. Who owns that pier is not yet confirmed.
- **Drone parts:** Ukraine's military intelligence found parts made by 4 Taiwan-listed, 1 Hong Kong-listed and 5 Shanghai- or Shenzhen-listed companies inside Iranian-designed Shahed and Geran drones. These are common commercial parts, and nothing shows the makers knew.

"Sanctioned" here means designated by the US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) under its Iran programs. These are US measures; buying Iranian oil is not illegal under Chinese law. Not investment advice.

## Hong Kong-listed companies that own sanctioned terminals

1. **Sinopec Kantons (HKEX 00934)** owns 50% of Rizhao Shihua, an oil terminal OFAC sanctioned on 2025-10-09 for unloading Iranian crude. Rizhao Shihua is being liquidated, and its piers and tanks were sold for about RMB 2.41bn to buyers Kantons calls only "independent third parties". Kantons' profit for the first half of 2026 fell 31% to HK$386M.
   - It still owns 50% of the main crude terminals at Qingdao and Ningbo, which are not sanctioned. Its five mainland terminal joint ventures earned HK$92.9M in the first half of 2026, down 52%.
   - Its wholly owned Huizhou terminal (Huade) unloaded 1.21M tonnes for an unnamed "third-party customer" in the same period, up 112%.
   - Sources: [profit warning](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0727/2026072700602.pdf), [liquidation notice](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0227/2026022702200.pdf), [2026 interim report](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0817/2026081701721.pdf)
2. **Qingdao Port (HKEX 06198, SSE 601298)** owns 70% of the sanctioned Dongjiakou oil terminal; its 2026 interim report does not mention the sanction ([report](http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-08-29/1225524260.PDF)). On 2025-12-12 it cancelled a RMB 1.79bn purchase of half of Rizhao Shihua, citing the US listing ([announcement](http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2025-12-12/1224871861.PDF)).
3. **PetroChina (HKEX 00857)** owns about 21% of the sanctioned Yangshan Shengang oil depot in Shanghai, a small part of its business.

Kantons and Qingdao Port disclosed the Rizhao sanction and its costs in their own filings. Mainland-only listings with sanctioned subsidiaries, such as Wintime Energy (SSE 600157, Huaying Huizhou terminal) and Hengli Petrochemical (SSE 600346, Dalian refinery), are in the [data](data/listed_company_links.csv).

## What the tracking data shows

We looked up 737 vessels on OFAC's Iran-related lists in [Global Fishing Watch](https://globalfishingwatch.org/), which records port visits from ships' location signals. It had 669 of them and about 220,000 dated port visits since 2017. Counts are of different ships:

| Port | Before the ship was sanctioned | After, last 12 months | After, tied up at a pier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ningbo | 271 | 7 | 0 |
| Qingdao | 157 | 0 | 0 |
| Dongjiakou | 135 | 1 | 0 |
| Rizhao | 78 | 0 | 0 |
| Tianjin, Caofeidian, Huizhou, Zhanjiang | 51 to 67 each | 0 | 0 |
| Chaozhou (Jinshiwan) | 29 | 7 | 7 |

The sanctioned crude tankers switch off their location signal before they go in, so this data can't show which crude terminal is still taking the oil.

**Chaozhou is the exception.** Seven sanctioned ships tied up there for one to three days each between 2025-10-27 and 2026-07-22, all after their sanction dates. Most are gas carriers (Cape Gas, Sapphire Gas, Gas Diana), so the cargo was probably liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), not crude. The berth sits beside three terminals: Chaozhou Asia-Pacific Fuel Storage, Ouhua Energy and a Huaying LNG terminal. The Huaying LNG terminal shares a name with the sanctioned Huaying Huizhou terminal, a Wintime Energy subsidiary, but we found no ownership link between the two. Which terminal the ships used, and who owns it, is unconfirmed.

In the last 12 months, the other sanctioned ships that kept their signal on were seen mostly in the UAE (204 ships), at anchorages off the Chinese coast such as Zhuhai, Shanghai and Hong Kong waters, in Iraq (62) and in India (59). Cargoes are often passed between ships at those anchorages.

**Taiwan:** 149 sanctioned tankers called there before they were sanctioned, and none after. No Taiwan-listed company or subsidiary is on OFAC's Iran lists.

**Korea and Japan:** 4 and 1 sanctioned tankers in the last 12 months. Malaysia, Indonesia, Singapore and Pakistan each had 1 to 4 tied up at a pier. Most of the other docked visits were at government-owned ports in Iraq, the UAE and Iran.

## Listed companies whose parts were found in Iranian drones

Ukraine's military intelligence (GUR) takes apart Iranian-designed Shahed-136, Shahed-107 and Geran drones shot down over Ukraine, and publishes each part's maker in its [components database](https://war-sanctions.gur.gov.ua/en/components). It names about 80 makers: 10 from Taiwan, the rest mostly from mainland China. The listed ones we matched:

- **Taiwan:** Delta Electronics (2308), Winbond Electronics (2344), Kaimei Electronic (2375, Jamicon capacitors) and PANJIT International (2481). Molicel, Minmax, Fotek, Oupiin, NAK and VBsemi are also named; we did not match them to a listing.
- **Hong Kong:** Man Yue Technology (HKEX 00894), a capacitor maker.
- **Shanghai and Shenzhen:** GigaDevice (603986), Shanghai Belling (600171), Yangjie Technology (300373), Hongfa (600885, Xiamen Hongfa relays) and Aihua (603989).

These are chips, capacitors, relays, diodes and power supplies sold in the millions through distributors. Finding a part in a drone shows it was bought, usually through resellers, not that the maker sold to Iran or knew. None of these companies is on OFAC's Iran lists. The risk to them is export-control action against their distributors and bad press, not a sanction on the company.

## How this could be taken further

1. **Chaozhou.** Match the seven visits to a specific berth, find who holds the port operating licence for it, and trace the owner to a listed parent. This is the only terminal-level lead the free data produced.
2. **Paid cargo trackers.** Kpler and Vortexa estimate where dark tankers unload, using satellite images and port agents. Kpler costs about $55,000 a year according to [Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/kpler). Their terminal-level figures, matched against the owners above, would show which listed companies still handle Iranian crude.
3. **Radar satellite images.** The EU's [Sentinel-1](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Copernicus/Sentinel-1) satellites photograph these coasts every few days, at night and through cloud, and the images are free. A large tanker at the Qingdao, Ningbo or Huizhou crude piers with no location signal is hiding where it is.
4. **Buyers.** Find who bought Rizhao Shihua's assets and who Huade's unnamed customer is, using Chinese court records, the [Shandong property rights exchange](http://www.sdcqjy.com/) and company registry records.

OFAC has sanctioned Chinese crude terminals one at a time, and the owners of the ones already hit have reported the damage. Free tracking data can't show which crude terminal took over Rizhao's cargoes. It does show sanctioned gas carriers still docking openly at Chaozhou, and that is the lead to follow first.
