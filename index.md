title: Sanctioned Iranian oil tankers vanish from tracking data at Chinese ports
---

> **AI-written, but the data is real.** Drafted by an AI agent (Devin) from public filings and public tracking data, and checked against the sources linked below. Not investment advice.

157 different US-sanctioned tankers called at Qingdao before they were sanctioned. In the last 12 months, after their sanctions, none did. Rizhao, Dongjiakou and Tianjin show the same drop, and at Ningbo the 7 that appeared never tied up at a pier. The tankers switch off their location signal before they go in. So public tracking data can show who owns the sanctioned terminals, but not which terminal is still taking the oil.

"Sanctioned" here means designated by the US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) under its Iran programs. These are US measures; buying Iranian oil is not illegal under Chinese law.

## Hong Kong-listed companies that own sanctioned terminals

1. **Sinopec Kantons (HKEX 00934)** owns 50% of Rizhao Shihua, an oil terminal OFAC sanctioned on 2025-10-09 for unloading Iranian crude. Rizhao Shihua is being liquidated, and its piers and tanks were sold for about RMB 2.41bn to buyers Kantons calls only "independent third parties". Kantons' profit for the first half of 2026 fell 31% to HK$386M.
   - It still owns 50% of the main crude terminals at Qingdao and Ningbo, which are not sanctioned. Its five mainland terminal joint ventures earned HK$92.9M in the first half of 2026, down 52%.
   - Its wholly owned Huizhou terminal (Huade) unloaded 1.21M tonnes for an unnamed "third-party customer" in the same period, up 112%.
   - Sources: [profit warning](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0727/2026072700602.pdf), [liquidation notice](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0227/2026022702200.pdf), [2026 interim report](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0817/2026081701721.pdf)
2. **Qingdao Port (HKEX 06198, SSE 601298)** owns 70% of the sanctioned Dongjiakou oil terminal; its 2026 interim report does not mention the sanction ([report](http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-08-29/1225524260.PDF)). On 2025-12-12 it cancelled a RMB 1.79bn purchase of half of Rizhao Shihua, citing the US listing ([announcement](http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2025-12-12/1224871861.PDF)).
3. **PetroChina (HKEX 00857)** owns about 21% of the sanctioned Yangshan Shengang oil depot in Shanghai, a small part of its business.

Kantons and Qingdao Port disclosed the Rizhao sanction and its costs in their own filings.

## What the tracking data shows

We looked up 737 vessels on OFAC's Iran-related lists in [Global Fishing Watch](https://globalfishingwatch.org/), which records port visits from ships' location signals. It had 669 of them and about 220,000 dated port visits since 2017. Counts are of different tankers:

| Port | Before the ship was sanctioned | After, last 12 months | After, tied up at a pier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ningbo | 271 | 7 | 0 |
| Qingdao | 157 | 0 | 0 |
| Dongjiakou | 135 | 1 | 0 |
| Rizhao | 78 | 0 | 0 |
| Tianjin, Caofeidian, Huizhou, Zhanjiang | 51 to 67 each | 0 | 0 |

In the last 12 months, the sanctioned tankers that kept their signal on were seen mostly in the UAE (204 ships), at anchorages off the Chinese coast such as Zhuhai, Shanghai and Hong Kong waters (76), in Iraq (62) and in India (59). Cargoes are often passed between ships at those anchorages.

**Taiwan:** 149 sanctioned tankers called there before they were sanctioned, and none after. No Taiwan-listed company or subsidiary is on OFAC's Iran lists. Ukraine's military intelligence [parts database](https://war-sanctions.gur.gov.ua/en/components) lists parts from Delta Electronics (2308) and PANJIT (2481) in Iranian-designed Shahed drones. These are common parts, usually bought through resellers, and nothing shows either company knew.

**Korea and Japan:** 4 and 1 sanctioned tankers in the last 12 months. Malaysia, Indonesia, Singapore and Pakistan each had 1 to 4 tied up at a pier. Most of the other docked visits were at government-owned ports in Iraq, the UAE and Iran.

## How this could be taken further

1. **Paid cargo trackers.** Kpler and Vortexa estimate where dark tankers unload, using satellite images and port agents. Kpler costs about $55,000 a year according to [Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/kpler). Their terminal-level figures, matched against the owners above, would show which listed companies still handle Iranian crude.
2. **Radar satellite images.** The EU's [Sentinel-1](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Copernicus/Sentinel-1) satellites photograph these coasts every few days, at night and through cloud, and the images are free. A large tanker at the Qingdao, Ningbo or Huizhou crude piers with no location signal is hiding where it is. Counting those before and after Rizhao was sanctioned would show whether its cargoes moved to another terminal.
3. **Buyers.** Find who bought Rizhao Shihua's assets and who Huade's unnamed customer is, using Chinese court records, the [Shandong property rights exchange](http://www.sdcqjy.com/) and customs data.

OFAC has sanctioned Chinese terminals one at a time, and the owners of the ones already hit have reported the damage. The open question is which terminal took over Rizhao's cargoes, and free location data can't answer it.
